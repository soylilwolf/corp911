Understanding Corporate Suspension

Learn what corporate suspension means and how to resolve it.

📋

What Is Corporate Suspension?

Corporate suspension is a legal status imposed by the state when a corporation fails to meet certain administrative or tax requirements. A suspended corporation loses many of its legal rights and protections, including the ability to:

  • Conduct business legally in the state
  • Enter into enforceable contracts
  • Initiate or maintain lawsuits
  • Sell or transfer corporate assets
  • Protect its corporate name
⚠

Common Reasons for Suspension

  • Failure to file annual reports – Most states require annual or biennial statements
  • Unpaid taxes or fees – State franchise taxes, LLC fees, or other state charges
  • Failure to maintain a registered agent – Every entity must have a registered agent on file
  • Non-compliance with state regulations – Missing required filings or licenses
  • Dissolved but not properly wound down – Administrative dissolution by the state
📄

How to Resolve Suspension

Reinstatement typically requires:

  1. Identify the reason – Check with the Secretary of State for the specific cause
  2. File missing documents – Submit any outstanding annual reports or statements
  3. Pay outstanding fees & taxes – Clear all unpaid taxes, penalties, and interest
  4. Submit a reinstatement application – File the appropriate reinstatement form
  5. Pay reinstatement fee – Varies by state, typically $25–$500
⏰

How Long Does Reinstatement Take?

Timelines vary by state and volume of filings:

  • Standard processing: 2–6 weeks
  • Expedited processing: 1–5 business days (where available)
  • Same-day service: Available in some states for additional fees
